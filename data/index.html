This firmware is an educational security research tool for the ESP32 microcontroller. Before using any feature, read and understand the following.

You Must Have Permission

You may only use this tool on networks and devices that you own or have received explicit, written authorisation to audit. Testing networks without permission is illegal in most countries, including under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA, US), the Computer Misuse Act (UK), the IT Act (India), and equivalent legislation worldwide.

What This Tool Can Do

ProjectHydraOS can perform deauthentication attacks, handshake capture, PMKID capture, Evil Twin attacks, beacon spam, BLE advertising, and rogue AP cloning. All of these techniques can disrupt real wireless networks and constitute criminal offences if used without authorisation.

Responsible Use

  • Only test in isolated lab environments or on your own equipment.
  • Never target public, commercial, or residential networks.
  • Do not use captures (PCAP, HCCAPX, PMKID) to access accounts or services you do not own.
  • Store captured data securely and delete it when no longer needed.
  • Be aware that 802.11 deauthentication affects all nearby devices on the channel.

Twin Deauth
Clones the target AP's BSSID and SSID on the same channel. Clients receive conflicting same network and disconnect. Very effective on every devices including modern phones with MFP support.
Packet Deauth
Sends raw 802.11 deauth frames. Supports multiple targets simultaneously. Devices with 802.11w (MFP) may resist.
Handshake Capture
Captures the WPA/WPA2 4-way handshake. Outputs .pcap and .hccapx for offline auditing with Hashcat or aircrack-ng or wordlist files.
PMKID Eapol frames
Captures the PMKID from the first EAPOL-Key frame — no connected client required. Works on most modern WPA2 APs.
Beacon Spam
Floods the air with fake beacon frames carrying random SSIDs, polluting Wi-Fi scan lists on nearby devices.
Evil Twin
Deploys an open clone of the target AP and starts deauth attack to the original. Victims connecting to the clone are redirected to a captive portal requesting the network password. Jamming continues until the correct password is submitted.
Probe Known Networks Spam
Exploits persistent device probing for saved networks. Clones those network names so devices attempt to connect to the ESP32 instead.

Deauth Attack Detector
Puts the ESP32 into promiscuous mode and monitors raw 802.11 management frames. Alerts when >10 deauth frames from a single BSSID are seen within 1 second. 00:00:00:00:00:00 indicates a broadcast deauth with no specific source — also a sign of an active attack.
